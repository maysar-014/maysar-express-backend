## PART I — CONSTITUTIONAL AUTHORITY

### Article 1. Purpose

This Constitution establishes the mandatory principles, financial controls, operational standards, technical requirements and governance mechanisms governing Maysar's delivery economics.

Its purpose is to ensure that every delivery transaction is commercially rational, operationally achievable, financially traceable and fair to all participating parties.

The framework governs:

- Customer delivery pricing and affordability.
- Driver compensation and earnings protection.
- Merchant contributions and commercial participation.
- Fulfillment and logistics expenditure.
- Payment processing and cash-on-delivery costs.
- Platform revenue, contribution margin and subsidies.
- Delivery density, batching and dispatch efficiency.
- Cancellations, failed deliveries and financial losses.
- Pricing configuration, approval and version management.
- Financial reconciliation, reporting and continuous improvement.

This Constitution applies to every delivery strategy, service area, merchant, driver, vehicle category and delivery transaction operated by Maysar.

### Article 2. Governing Philosophy

Maysar shall build a delivery ecosystem in which commercial sustainability and participant fairness reinforce one another.

No participant shall routinely finance the commercial weakness of another participant through hidden losses, unreasonable charges, suppressed compensation or undisclosed deductions.

The following principles are mandatory:

1. **Buyer fairness**: Delivery prices must be reasonable, transparent and proportionate to the service.
2. **Driver dignity**: Driver compensation must reflect the time, distance, effort, risk and operating costs associated with the delivery.
3. **Merchant fairness**: Merchant contributions must be transparent, commercially justified and consistently applied.
4. **Platform sustainability**: Maysar must earn sufficient contribution to fund its operations and future development.
5. **Financial integrity**: Every material cost, payment, subsidy and revenue allocation must be traceable.
6. **Operational efficiency**: Coordination and utilization improvements should be preferred over simply transferring costs to buyers or drivers.
7. **Evidence-based decisions**: Pricing and operational decisions must rely on verified data, documented assumptions or explicitly approved estimates.

### Article 3. Fundamental Economic Objective

Every delivery must be evaluated across four dimensions:

- Buyer affordability.
- Driver economic attractiveness.
- Merchant commercial viability.
- Maysar financial sustainability.

A delivery shall not be considered economically healthy merely because the buyer pays a fee or the platform records revenue. The complete transaction must be evaluated against its actual financial obligations.

## PART II — DELIVERY ECONOMIC ARCHITECTURE

### Article 4. Independent Economic Engines

Maysar shall maintain logically independent but financially integrated economic engines.

#### 4.1 Customer Pricing Engine

Responsible for calculating the amount presented to the buyer. It shall consider:

- Delivery distance.
- Delivery strategy.
- Delivery zone.
- Expected travel conditions.
- Package weight.
- Volumetric weight where applicable.
- Service level.
- Intercity corridor costs.
- Applicable customer surcharges.
- Approved promotions or discounts.
- Pricing minimums and maximums.

The Customer Pricing Engine shall not independently determine whether a delivery is profitable.

#### 4.2 Driver Earnings Engine

Responsible for determining the driver's compensation. It shall consider:

- Pickup and delivery distance.
- Estimated and actual travel duration.
- Waiting time.
- Traffic conditions.
- Vehicle type.
- Delivery complexity.
- Package characteristics.
- Peak-period incentives.
- Remote-area compensation.
- Cancellation compensation where applicable.
- Applicable contractual guarantees.

Driver compensation shall be based on a documented compensation policy rather than an arbitrary residual from the customer delivery fee.

#### 4.3 Fulfillment Cost Engine

Responsible for identifying the actual variable cost of completing a delivery. Potential cost categories include:

- Driver compensation.
- Third-party logistics charges.
- Line-haul transportation.
- Pickup and destination handling.
- Payment processing.
- COD collection and reconciliation.
- Packaging or handling obligations where applicable.
- Insurance or delivery-risk expenses.
- Other directly attributable variable costs.

The engine must prevent double-counting. Where the driver payout already covers last-mile delivery, the same last-mile expense must not also be added as an independent fulfillment cost.

#### 4.4 Merchant Contribution Engine

Responsible for calculating merchant-funded delivery support. Merchant contributions must be:

- Explicitly agreed.
- Contractually or commercially documented.
- Recorded against the relevant transaction.
- Reconciled against actual collected amounts.

Expected merchant contributions must never be treated as collected revenue before they are confirmed.

#### 4.5 Subsidy and Partnership Engine

Responsible for approved financial support from Maysar, merchants, commercial partners, promotional campaigns and other authorized funding sources.

Every subsidy must have a defined funding source, purpose, amount, authorization and expiry or review condition.

#### 4.6 Financial Feasibility Engine

This is the final authority for determining whether a delivery quote is financially acceptable.

It must reconcile all revenue sources, compensation commitments, fulfillment expenses and approved subsidies before a quote is authorized.

## PART III — FINANCIAL GOVERNANCE

### Article 5. Mandatory Financial Model

Every delivery transaction shall be evaluated using the following structure.

#### 5.1 Total Delivery Funding

Total Delivery Funding consists of:

- Customer delivery payment.
- Merchant delivery contribution.
- Approved partner contribution.
- Approved platform-funded subsidy.

These sources must be recorded separately.

#### 5.2 Total Delivery Cost

Total Delivery Cost consists of all attributable delivery obligations, including:

- Driver payout.
- Third-party transportation.
- Handling and line-haul expenses.
- Payment processing costs.
- COD collection expenses.
- Other applicable variable delivery expenses.

Each cost must have a defined accounting classification to prevent duplication.

#### 5.3 Platform Contribution

```
Platform Contribution = Total Delivery Funding − Total Delivery Cost
```

This amount represents the delivery transaction's contribution before allocating fixed corporate overhead, unless the report explicitly includes those overhead allocations.

#### 5.4 Contribution Margin

```
Contribution Margin (%) = Platform Contribution ÷ Total Delivery Funding × 100
```

Where total funding is zero, the margin percentage must be reported as undefined rather than forced into a misleading value.

#### 5.5 Economic Classification

Every delivery must be assigned one of the following classifications:

| Classification | Definition |
|---------------|------------|
| `PROFITABLE` | Positive platform contribution. |
| `BREAK_EVEN` | Zero platform contribution. |
| `SUBSIDIZED` | A delivery supported by an explicitly approved subsidy. |
| `LOSS_MAKING` | Negative contribution not adequately covered by approved funding. |
| `UNVERIFIED` | Insufficient or incomplete financial evidence. |

A subsidized delivery must also retain its underlying pre-subsidy economic result. Subsidization must not conceal the underlying cost structure.

### Article 6. Markup versus Margin

Maysar shall distinguish between markup and margin.

A percentage added to fulfillment cost is a markup, not automatically a profit margin on revenue. Pricing configuration and financial reporting must identify the formula being used. No financial dashboard shall label a cost-based markup as a contribution margin.

### Article 7. No Hidden Losses

The following practices are prohibited:

- Calling a delivery profitable when driver compensation is excluded.
- Treating uncollected merchant contributions as actual revenue.
- Ignoring payment processing or COD expenses.
- Hiding subsidies inside customer pricing.
- Allowing price ceilings to create undisclosed funding deficits.
- Counting the same logistics expense twice.
- Reporting estimated costs as actual expenditure.
- Treating customer delivery payments as platform profit.

Any financial result with incomplete cost coverage must be classified as `UNVERIFIED`.

## PART IV — CUSTOMER PRICING AND AFFORDABILITY

### Article 8. Customer Pricing Principles

Customer delivery pricing must balance affordability, service quality and the economic realities of fulfillment. The pricing system shall:

1. Use distance-sensitive pricing rather than relying exclusively on a universal flat fee.
2. Reflect meaningful differences between delivery strategies and geographic areas.
3. Avoid unreasonable price jumps between adjacent distance bands.
4. Apply weight and volumetric surcharges only where justified.
5. Distinguish ordinary customer charges from optional services.
6. Present all applicable charges before order confirmation.
7. Maintain an auditable record of the pricing calculation.

### Article 9. Customer Price Ceilings

A customer price ceiling is an affordability control, not permission to underfund delivery.

When the calculated customer price exceeds an approved ceiling, the system must:

- Identify the resulting funding gap.
- Determine whether an authorized merchant or platform subsidy can cover it.
- Consider a different delivery option where available.
- Request additional authorization where necessary.
- Decline to offer the delivery where it cannot be funded responsibly.

The system must never silently reduce the price while leaving the driver or another participant to absorb the deficit.

### Article 10. Affordability Measurement

Maysar shall monitor:

- Average delivery fee.
- Median delivery fee.
- Delivery fee as a proportion of order value.
- Delivery fee by distance band.
- Checkout abandonment associated with delivery pricing, where measurable.
- Repeat purchase rates.
- Customer complaints concerning delivery charges.
- Price differences between zones and strategies.

Order-value ratios must be interpreted carefully. A low-value order can have a high delivery-fee ratio even where the absolute fee is reasonable. Affordability decisions must therefore use multiple indicators rather than one universal threshold.

## PART V — DRIVER ECONOMIC PROTECTION

### Article 11. Driver Compensation Policy

Driver compensation shall be independently calculated and financially protected. The compensation policy must consider:

- Distance travelled.
- Travel duration.
- Fuel and vehicle operating expenses.
- Vehicle category.
- Waiting and loading time.
- Traffic exposure.
- Delivery complexity.
- Peak demand.
- Geographic accessibility.
- Return-trip and repositioning realities.
- Applicable cancellation obligations.

The policy must distinguish driver gross payout from driver net earnings.

### Article 12. Driver Net Earnings

Maysar shall monitor driver economics using:

```
Driver Net Earnings = Driver Gross Earnings − Driver-Borne Operating Expenses
```

Operating expenses may include fuel, maintenance, vehicle rental, financing and other legitimate driver-borne costs. Maysar shall not assume that the gross payout represents the driver's actual earnings.

### Article 13. Driver Hourly Earnings

The platform shall report:

- Earnings per active delivery hour.
- Earnings per online hour.
- Earnings per completed delivery.
- Average waiting time.
- Deliveries per active hour.
- Cancellation compensation.
- Peak-period earnings.
- Net earnings by vehicle category and delivery zone where data supports it.

Both active-hour and online-hour measures are important. Active-hour earnings show productivity while engaged in deliveries. Online-hour earnings reveal the economic reality of time spent waiting for work.

### Article 14. Driver Competitiveness

Maysar may compare its driver economics against alternative delivery and ride-hailing opportunities using legitimate market research and verified driver feedback. Comparisons must account for:

- Net rather than gross earnings.
- Hours actually worked.
- Vehicle expenses.
- Waiting time.
- Work availability.
- Incentive reliability.
- Payment timing.
- Geographic demand.

Maysar shall not promise superior earnings without evidence.

### Article 15. Driver Compensation Protection

No delivery may be approved if its funding arrangement violates the applicable driver compensation commitment.

Where a delivery is subsidized, the driver must receive the agreed compensation regardless of whether the customer or merchant funds the full amount. Any proposed change to driver compensation requires documented economic justification, review and approval.

## PART VI — MERCHANT CONTRIBUTIONS

### Article 16. Merchant Participation

Merchant-funded delivery contributions may improve affordability and delivery sustainability. However, contributions must be based on transparent commercial arrangements.

Maysar shall distinguish:

- Merchant commissions.
- Merchant-funded delivery contributions.
- Merchant promotions.
- Merchant-funded customer discounts.
- Other commercial revenue.

These categories must not be combined indiscriminately.

### Article 17. Merchant Fairness

Merchant contributions shall be evaluated according to:

- Merchant category.
- Order economics.
- Average order value.
- Delivery frequency.
- Geographic location.
- Commercial agreement.
- Merchant retention.
- Incremental sales generated.

No merchant shall be charged an undisclosed delivery contribution. Maysar shall monitor whether contribution requirements materially undermine merchant profitability or participation.

## PART VII — DELIVERY STRATEGIES AND OPERATIONAL EFFICIENCY

### Article 18. Strategy Governance

Every delivery must be assigned an approved strategy. The pricing and financial systems shall support, where applicable:

| Strategy | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| `LOCAL_URBAN` | Short-distance urban delivery. |
| `METROPOLITAN` | Medium-distance metropolitan delivery. |
| `INTERCITY` | Long-distance intercity delivery. |
| `REMOTE` | Remote or rural area delivery. |

Each strategy must have independently configurable cost assumptions, eligibility rules, service levels and financial thresholds.

### Article 19. Distance-Band Governance

Distance bands shall be reviewed using actual delivery evidence. For each band, Maysar shall measure:

- Completed deliveries.
- Average and median distance.
- Average duration.
- Driver payout.
- Customer fee.
- Merchant contribution.
- Variable cost.
- Platform contribution.
- Cancellation and failure rates.

Distance-band boundaries must be reviewed when they create persistent pricing distortions or financial losses.

### Article 20. Intercity and Remote Deliveries

Intercity pricing shall separately identify:

- Origin handling.
- Line-haul transportation.
- Destination handling.
- Final-mile delivery.
- Applicable weight or volumetric charges.
- Additional service costs.

Corridor configuration must explicitly define directionality, minimum charges, capacity restrictions and service availability. Remote deliveries may be marked unavailable when Maysar cannot establish reliable fulfillment or responsible funding.

### Article 21. Delivery Density and Batching

Maysar shall prioritize operational efficiency as a means of improving delivery economics. The platform shall evaluate:

- Deliveries per driver-hour.
- Orders per geographic zone.
- Average distance between consecutive assignments.
- Pickup proximity.
- Merchant preparation time.
- Driver idle time.
- Batch utilization.
- Additional distance created by batching.
- Customer delivery-time impact.

Batching shall only be used where it improves total system efficiency without unfairly compromising driver compensation, package integrity or delivery commitments. A batch must not be treated as multiple independent deliveries for compensation purposes unless the driver compensation policy explicitly accounts for the combined work.

### Article 22. Coordination Standards

Operational improvements should prioritize:

1. Accurate merchant preparation-time estimates.
2. Intelligent driver assignment.
3. Pickup proximity.
4. Geographic clustering.
5. Reduced unnecessary repositioning.
6. Appropriate batch formation.
7. Reliable route estimation.
8. Faster order handover.
9. Better customer availability and communication.

Efficiency gains must be measured against actual costs and service outcomes.

## PART VIII — CANCELLATIONS, FAILURES AND RISK

### Article 23. Delivery Outcome Classification

Every delivery attempt must have a traceable outcome. The mandatory outcome set is:

| Outcome | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| `COMPLETED` | Delivery successfully delivered. |
| `CANCELLED_BY_CUSTOMER` | Customer cancelled before dispatch or delivery. |
| `CANCELLED_BY_MERCHANT` | Merchant cancelled the order or fulfillment. |
| `CANCELLED_BY_DRIVER` | Driver cancelled the assignment. |
| `CANCELLED_BY_PLATFORM` | Platform-initiated cancellation. |
| `FAILED_CUSTOMER_UNAVAILABLE` | Customer not reachable or not present. |
| `FAILED_MERCHANT_UNAVAILABLE` | Merchant not ready or unavailable at pickup. |
| `FAILED_ADDRESS_OR_LOCATION` | Delivery location could not be found or accessed. |
| `FAILED_OTHER` | Other failure cause. |
| `RETURNED_TO_ORIGIN` | Package returned after failed delivery. |

The system may use additional operational classifications where necessary.

### Article 24. Cancellation Economics

Maysar shall identify the financial impact of each unsuccessful delivery. Relevant costs include:

- Driver compensation already earned.
- Pickup or return journey.
- Waiting time.
- Handling costs.
- Payment fees.
- Refund expenses.
- Product damage or loss where applicable.

Cancellation fees must be governed by transparent policies and must not automatically transfer every failure cost to the buyer. Responsibility must be established using evidence.

### Article 25. Failed Delivery Controls

Maysar shall monitor:

- Failure rate.
- Return-to-origin rate.
- Cost per failed attempt.
- Repeat failure causes.
- Fraud and abuse indicators.
- COD discrepancies.
- Customer and merchant dispute rates.

Corrective measures should target recurring operational causes rather than relying solely on penalties.

## PART IX — CASH ON DELIVERY AND PAYMENT ECONOMICS

### Article 26. Payment Cost Governance

Payment processing costs must be recorded by payment method and transaction type. Maysar shall distinguish:

- Mobile money.
- Card payments.
- Bank transfers.
- Cash payments.
- COD collection.
- COD remittance.
- Refunds and reversals.

The platform must reconcile the amount collected, the amount owed to each party and the amount actually settled.

### Article 27. COD Controls

Cash on delivery introduces collection, reconciliation and settlement risks. The COD framework shall establish:

- Collection responsibility.
- Proof of collection.
- Transaction references.
- Cash handover procedures.
- Settlement deadlines.
- Discrepancy reporting.
- Refund and reversal procedures.
- Escalation for missing funds.

COD charges must reflect actual service costs and documented risk, not arbitrary revenue targets.

### Article 28. COD Financial Integrity

The system shall never treat an order marked as delivered as sufficient evidence that cash was successfully collected and reconciled.

**Delivery completion, payment collection and financial settlement are separate events.**

## PART X — TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE AND DATA INTEGRITY

### Article 29. Pricing Engine Requirements

The delivery pricing engine shall evolve into a financially complete pricing architecture. The existing implementation provides useful foundations:

- Strategy-aware pricing.
- Distance-band pricing.
- Weight surcharges.
- Peak-hour pricing.
- Intercity corridor calculations.
- COD charges.
- Pricing floors and ceilings.
- Explicit route source.
- Pricing version identification.
- Subsidy calculation.

However, these capabilities do not independently establish actual delivery profitability. The following corrections are mandatory:

1. Driver compensation must be included in financial feasibility.
2. Merchant contributions must be separately represented.
3. All applicable variable costs must be included.
4. A ceiling must not silently conceal a funding deficit.
5. Markup and margin must be correctly distinguished.
6. Duration must contribute to driver economics.
7. Package dimensions must support volumetric-weight calculation where relevant.
8. All monetary inputs and configuration values must be validated.
9. Invalid distances, negative values, `NaN` and `Infinity` must be rejected.
10. Missing or invalid corridor configuration must fail safely.
11. Rounding increments must be validated.
12. Customer charges and driver incentives must remain independently configurable.

### Article 30. Financial Feasibility Result

The financial feasibility layer shall return a structured result containing at minimum:

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| `customerDeliveryFee` | Amount presented to and paid by the buyer. |
| `merchantContribution` | Confirmed merchant-funded delivery support. |
| `partnerContribution` | Confirmed partner-funded contribution. |
| `platformSubsidy` | Explicitly approved Maysar-funded subsidy. |
| `driverPayout` | Driver gross compensation commitment. |
| `otherFulfillmentExpenses` | Other attributable variable costs. |
| `paymentExpenses` | Payment processing costs. |
| `codExpenses` | COD collection and reconciliation costs. |
| `totalFunding` | Sum of all funding sources. |
| `totalVariableCosts` | Sum of all variable delivery costs. |
| `platformContribution` | `totalFunding − totalVariableCosts`. |
| `contributionMargin` | `platformContribution ÷ totalFunding × 100`. |
| `economicClassification` | One of the five mandatory classifications. |
| `pricingVersion` | Pricing configuration version identifier. |
| `costModelVersion` | Cost model version identifier. |
| `financialAssumptions` | Documented assumptions used in the calculation. |
| `validationWarnings` | Non-fatal warnings for downstream review. |
| `quoteExpiry` | Timestamp after which the quote is no longer valid. |

The result must be deterministic for identical inputs and identical configuration versions.

### Article 31. Immutable Quote Snapshots

Every accepted delivery quote must preserve:

- Original customer price.
- Driver compensation commitment.
- Merchant funding commitment.
- Subsidy allocation.
- Delivery strategy.
- Distance and route assumptions.
- Cost assumptions.
- Pricing version.
- Quote timestamp.
- Expiry and acceptance timestamps.

Changes to future pricing configuration must not retroactively alter accepted financial commitments.

### Article 32. Actual-versus-Quoted Reconciliation

After each completed or unsuccessful delivery, Maysar shall compare:

- Quoted distance versus actual distance.
- Estimated duration versus actual duration.
- Estimated driver compensation versus actual payout.
- Estimated fulfillment cost versus actual cost.
- Expected merchant contribution versus collected contribution.
- Expected payment cost versus actual payment cost.
- Expected platform contribution versus realized contribution.

Material differences must be classified and investigated.

### Article 33. Auditability

Every financial adjustment must have:

- A transaction reference.
- A reason.
- An amount.
- A timestamp.
- An initiating actor.
- An approving actor where required.
- An associated configuration or policy version.

No material financial record may be silently overwritten.

## PART XI — FINANCIAL PERFORMANCE GOVERNANCE

### Article 34. Mandatory Executive Dashboard

Maysar shall maintain a delivery economics dashboard covering the following indicators.

#### A. Customer Economics
- Average and median delivery fee.
- Delivery fee by distance band.
- Delivery fee by strategy.
- Delivery fee as a proportion of order value.
- Checkout abandonment associated with delivery charges.
- Customer delivery complaints.

#### B. Driver Economics
- Average gross payout.
- Estimated and actual net earnings.
- Net earnings per active hour.
- Net earnings per online hour.
- Average waiting time.
- Deliveries per active hour.
- Driver retention.
- Compensation disputes.

#### C. Merchant Economics
- Merchant contributions collected.
- Expected versus actual contributions.
- Merchant-funded promotions.
- Contribution by merchant category.
- Merchant retention and participation.

#### D. Platform Economics
- Total delivery funding.
- Total variable fulfillment costs.
- Platform contribution per completed order.
- Contribution margin.
- Subsidy per order.
- Subsidy by funding source.
- Loss-making delivery percentage.
- Cost of failed deliveries.

#### E. Operational Efficiency
- Delivery completion rate.
- Cancellation rate.
- Failed delivery rate.
- Return-to-origin rate.
- Average delivery duration.
- Average distance.
- Deliveries per driver-hour.
- Batch utilization.
- Merchant preparation time.
- Driver idle time.

Every metric must have a defined formula, source, reporting period and data-quality status.

### Article 35. Mandatory Segmentation

Where evidence supports it, all relevant metrics must be segmented by:

- Delivery strategy.
- Distance band.
- Vehicle category.
- Geographic zone.
- Time of day.
- Day of week.
- Peak versus ordinary periods.
- Merchant category.
- Payment method.
- Delivery outcome.

Insufficient data must be reported explicitly. Small samples must not be presented as statistically reliable trends.

## PART XII — MONTHLY REVIEW AND REPORTING

### Article 36. Monthly Delivery Economics Review

Maysar shall conduct a formal monthly review. The review must cover:

1. Customer delivery affordability.
2. Driver payouts and net earnings per active hour.
3. Merchant contributions.
4. Fulfillment and payment costs.
5. COD costs and reconciliation.
6. Subsidies and funding sources.
7. Contribution margin per completed delivery.
8. Delivery density and batching.
9. Cancellations and failed deliveries.
10. Quoted estimates versus actual costs.
11. Segment-level financial performance.
12. Comparison with previous reporting periods.

### Article 37. Reporting Standards

Every monthly report must contain:

- Reporting period.
- Data coverage.
- Executive findings.
- Supporting financial metrics.
- Methodology and calculation definitions.
- Actual versus estimated values.
- Missing or unreliable data.
- Trends compared with previous periods.
- Loss-making and underfunded routes.
- Driver compensation risks.
- Customer affordability concerns.
- Prioritized corrective actions.
- Responsible owners.
- Decisions requiring management approval.

A report must still be produced when there were no completed deliveries, data is unchanged, financial data is unavailable, the platform remains pre-launch, or the available sample is insufficient. The report must explain the limitation rather than silently skip the review.

| **First review** | 1 December 2026 |

## PART XIII — APPROVALS, RESPONSIBILITIES AND CHANGE CONTROL

### Article 39. Governance Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibilities |
|------|-----------------|
| **Executive Management** | Delivery economic strategy; major subsidy commitments; commercial priorities; participant-economics conflicts; material pricing policy changes. |
| **Finance** | Cost verification; payment reconciliation; merchant contribution verification; driver payout reconciliation; subsidy accounting; contribution margin validation; monthly financial reporting. |
| **Operations & Logistics** | Driver coordination; merchant preparation performance; delivery density; dispatch efficiency; cancellation investigations; route and fulfillment performance. |
| **Engineering** | Pricing engine implementation; financial feasibility controls; immutable quote snapshots; data integrity; audit trails; reporting pipelines; automated validation and monitoring. |
| **Commercial & Merchant Management** | Merchant agreements; merchant contribution policies; customer affordability; market competitiveness; driver and merchant feedback. |

One person may hold multiple responsibilities during the pre-launch stage, but accountability must remain explicit.

### Article 40. Approval Controls

The following require documented approval before deployment:

- New delivery pricing strategies.
- Material price-band changes.
- Driver compensation policy changes.
- New subsidies or subsidy extensions.
- Changes to customer price ceilings.
- New intercity corridors.
- Material merchant contribution changes.
- Changes to COD charges.
- Changes to financial feasibility thresholds.

The approving authority must be defined before the relevant change is deployed.

### Article 41. Pricing Configuration Governance

Pricing configuration must be:

- Version-controlled.
- Validated before activation.
- Tested against representative scenarios.
- Approved before production deployment.
- Logged with an effective date.
- Reversible through a documented rollback process.

No production pricing change may rely solely on an undocumented manual database edit.

## PART XIV — RISK MANAGEMENT AND EXCEPTION CONTROL

### Article 42. Financial Risk Categories

Maysar shall monitor at least the following risks:

- Driver underpayment.
- Unfunded customer price ceilings.
- Persistent route-level losses.
- Uncollected merchant contributions.
- Payment settlement discrepancies.
- COD leakage.
- Excessive failed deliveries.
- Uncontrolled promotional subsidies.
- Low delivery density.
- Incorrect distance estimates.
- Incorrect cost configuration.
- Fraudulent delivery completion.
- Double-counted or omitted costs.

### Article 43. Exception Escalation

A delivery or route must be escalated when:

- Its guaranteed driver compensation cannot be funded.
- Its expected funding is insufficient.
- Its actual cost materially exceeds the quote.
- Its corridor or zone lacks validated cost assumptions.
- Its financial classification is unreliable.
- Its COD reconciliation is incomplete.
- Its operational risk exceeds approved limits.

The system must distinguish a temporary exception from a recurring structural problem.

### Article 44. Controlled Experimentation

Maysar may test alternative delivery prices, compensation arrangements, batching rules and subsidies through controlled pilots.

Every pilot must define:

- Business hypothesis.
- Eligible zones and participants.
- Duration.
- Expected financial impact.
- Customer and driver protection measures.
- Success criteria.
- Failure criteria.
- Data collection requirements.
- Approval authority.

Experiments must not compromise already accepted delivery commitments.

## PART XV — IMPLEMENTATION ROADMAP

### Article 45. Phase 1 — Establish Financial Visibility

**Priority: Critical**

- Identify all existing delivery-related data sources.
- Define the transaction-level financial data model.
- Establish customer, merchant, driver and platform funding categories.
- Identify all variable delivery costs.
- Define authoritative calculation formulas.
- Establish data-quality checks.
- Produce a baseline financial report.

**Exit condition:** Maysar can reconstruct the financial position of an individual delivery from its records.

### Article 46. Phase 2 — Correct the Pricing Architecture

**Priority: Critical**

- Separate customer pricing from driver compensation.
- Implement fulfillment cost accounting.
- Implement merchant contribution accounting.
- Implement subsidy governance.
- Implement the financial feasibility engine.
- Correct margin and markup calculations.
- Introduce explicit funding-deficit outcomes.
- Implement immutable quote snapshots.

**Exit condition:** No delivery can be approved using an incomplete or silently underfunded financial calculation.

### Article 47. Phase 3 — Validate Driver and Customer Economics

**Priority: High**

- Establish driver compensation assumptions.
- Collect real operating-cost evidence.
- Measure active and online earnings.
- Validate customer affordability.
- Compare driver economics against legitimate alternatives.
- Validate merchant contribution arrangements.

**Exit condition:** The delivery model has evidence-based compensation and affordability policies.

### Article 48. Phase 4 — Conduct Controlled Delivery Pilots

**Priority: High**

- Select representative delivery zones.
- Test multiple distance bands.
- Test different vehicle categories.
- Measure actual travel time and cost.
- Evaluate batching.
- Reconcile quoted and actual delivery economics.
- Review cancellation and failure costs.

**Exit condition:** The model demonstrates acceptable economic performance under real operating conditions.

### Article 49. Phase 5 — Controlled Commercial Expansion

Expansion shall be based on verified results. A zone, corridor or delivery strategy may expand only when:

- Fulfillment capacity is reliable.
- Driver compensation is sustainable.
- Customer pricing is commercially reasonable.
- Financial reporting is complete enough to support decisions.
- Subsidies are explicitly funded.
- Material operational risks are controlled.

## PART XVI — NON-NEGOTIABLE ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

### Article 50. Conditions for Commercial Approval

The following conditions are mandatory:

| # | Condition |
|---|-----------|
| 1 | No delivery is classified as profitable using incomplete cost data. |
| 2 | No approved delivery violates its guaranteed driver compensation commitment. |
| 3 | No customer price ceiling silently creates an unfunded delivery. |
| 4 | No merchant contribution is counted before verification. |
| 5 | Every subsidy has an identified funding source and approval. |
| 6 | Every accepted quote preserves its original financial commitments. |
| 7 | Every completed delivery can be reconciled against actual expenditure. |
| 8 | Every material financial adjustment is auditable. |
| 9 | Invalid pricing inputs cannot produce an approved quote. |
| 10 | Pricing changes are tested and version-controlled. |
| 11 | Failed deliveries and cancellations have traceable financial outcomes. |
| 12 | Monthly reports identify missing data and reporting limitations. |
| 13 | Delivery expansion is supported by measurable operating evidence. |
| 14 | Customer, merchant and driver fairness remain explicit decision criteria. |

A failure against a critical criterion must block the affected pricing configuration, delivery strategy or commercial expansion until the risk is resolved or formally accepted by an authorized executive under a documented exception process.

## PART XVII — CONSTITUTIONAL REVIEW AND AMENDMENTS

### Article 51. Continuous Review

This Constitution shall be reviewed:

- Monthly through the delivery economics review.
- Whenever a material delivery strategy is introduced.
- Whenever a significant financial or operational risk emerges.
- Whenever the pricing or compensation architecture materially changes.
- Before major commercial expansion.

### Article 52. Amendment Procedure

Every amendment must contain:

- The existing rule.
- The proposed change.
- The business justification.
- Financial implications.
- Customer impact.
- Driver impact.
- Merchant impact.
- Technical implications.
- Approval record.
- Effective date.

No amendment may weaken fundamental financial integrity or remove traceability without explicit executive authorization and documented risk acceptance.

## FINAL CONSTITUTIONAL DECLARATION

Maysar's delivery system shall not be governed by the pursuit of the lowest possible delivery price, the highest possible platform margin or the largest possible driver payout in isolation.

It shall be governed by the creation of a balanced, transparent and sustainable delivery economy.

> Every delivery must have a clear economic purpose, a defensible price, a funded fulfillment commitment, fair participant treatment and an auditable financial outcome.

Maysar shall pursue **efficiency before cost shifting**, **evidence before assumptions**, **transparency before hidden subsidies** and **sustainable growth before premature expansion**.

The governing standard is:

*A delivery is not commercially successful merely because it is completed. It is successful when the customer receives fair value, the driver receives fair compensation, the merchant participates under reasonable terms and Maysar can sustainably fulfill its obligations.*
